# 1. Absolute selection in the full central boundary

`findings-63.md` proves finite identification inside each fixed ordered-block family. The present result removes that family restriction for deterministic ordered-layer sectors. A finite set of ordinary induced-DAG densities isolates the target among all central measures on the DAG branching graph, including nonextremal mixtures and sectors with no a priori finite-layer representation.

Fix an integer $q\geq1$ and a positive probability vector

```math
p=(p_1,\ldots,p_q),
\qquad
p_i>0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^q p_i=1.
```

The target sector $\mu^p$ is generated by independent layer marks with law $p$, with every vertex in layer $i$ pointing to every vertex in layer $j$ whenever $i<j$. No edges occur inside a layer.

# 2. Two forbidden three-vertex patterns

Let $A$ denote the three-vertex DAG consisting of one directed edge and one isolated vertex. Let $P$ denote the open directed path

```math
1\longrightarrow2\longrightarrow3
```

with no edge from $1$ to $3$.

## Proposition 1. Strict weak-order criterion

A finite or countable DAG contains no induced copy of $A$ or $P$ if and only if its incomparability relation is an equivalence relation and the corresponding equivalence classes are linearly ordered, with every possible edge directed from each lower class to each higher class.

## Proof

The absence of $P$ makes the edge relation transitive. The absence of $A$ makes incomparability transitive: if $x$ is incomparable with $y$, and $y$ is incomparable with $z$, then comparability of $x$ and $z$ would induce $A$. Incomparability is already reflexive and symmetric, so it is an equivalence relation.

Distinct equivalence classes are pairwise comparable. Transitivity and acyclicity make the direction independent of the chosen representatives and linearly order the quotient. Every cross-class pair is therefore joined in the same direction. The converse is immediate.

The resulting object is a strict weak order, equivalently a complete ordered multipartite DAG.

# 3. Rooted degree-pair coordinates

For an extremal central sector, choose a uniformly rooted vertex in its exchangeable infinite realization. Let

```math
D^-
```

and

```math
D^+
```

be its limiting incoming and outgoing vertex frequencies. Write $\nu$ for the law of $(D^-,D^+)$ on $[0,1]^2$.

Its mixed moments are finite relational observables. If $B_{a,b}$ is the directed bi-star with $a$ leaves pointing into the root and $b$ leaves pointed to by the root, then

```math
\int x^a y^b\,d\nu(x,y)
=
t(B_{a,b}),
```

where the right side is the homomorphism density of $B_{a,b}$. Each such homomorphism density is a finite linear combination of induced-DAG densities on $a+b+1$ vertices.

For the target sector, set

```math
a_i=\sum_{j<i}p_j,
\qquad
b_i=\sum_{j>i}p_j.
```

A vertex in layer $i$ has degree pair $(a_i,b_i)$, and

```math
1-a_i-b_i=p_i.
```

# 4. The forcing polynomial

Define the nonnegative polynomial

```math
Q_p(x,y)
=
\prod_{i=1}^q
\left(
(x-a_i)^2+(y-b_i)^2
\right).
```

Its zero set is exactly

```math
Z_p=\{(a_i,b_i):1\leq i\leq q\}.
```

The expectation

```math
\mathcal Q_p(\mu)
=
\int Q_p(x,y)\,d\nu_\mu(x,y)
```

is a finite linear combination of directed bi-star densities of total leaf number at most $2q$. It can therefore be written as a finite linear combination of induced-DAG densities on at most $2q+1$ vertices.

# 5. Absolute finite-forcing theorem

## Theorem 2. Absolute forcing of deterministic ordered layers

Let $\mu$ be any central measure on the DAG branching graph. Assume

```math
\mu_3(A)=0,
\qquad
\mu_3(P)=0,
```

and

```math
\mathcal Q_p(\mu)=0.
```

Then

```math
\mu=\mu^p.
```

Consequently, every deterministic finite ordered-layer sector is absolutely finitely forcible in the full central DAG boundary. It is enough to prescribe induced-DAG densities on at most $2q+1$ vertices.

## Proof

Decompose $\mu$ into extremal central sectors. Since the two forbidden-pattern densities are nonnegative and vanish in the mixture, they vanish in almost every extremal component. Proposition 1 therefore makes almost every component a strict weak-order sector.

Likewise, $Q_p\geq0$ and $\mathcal Q_p(\mu)=0$ imply

```math
Q_p(D^-,D^+)=0
```

almost surely in almost every component. Hence every positive-frequency incomparability class has degree pair in $Z_p$.

In a strict weak order, a class with incoming frequency $a$ and outgoing frequency $b$ has asymptotic mass

```math
1-a-b.
```

Thus a class at $(a_i,b_i)$ has mass exactly $p_i$. Two distinct classes cannot have the same degree pair. If an extremal component contains the subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,q\}$ of the target degree pairs, its total vertex mass is therefore

```math
\sum_{i\in S}p_i.
```

The degree-pair law has no support outside $Z_p$, so this sum must equal one. Positivity of every $p_i$ forces $S=\{1,\ldots,q\}$. The component is therefore precisely the target ordered-layer sector. Almost every extremal component is the same, so the original central measure is $\mu^p$.

# 6. Ordinary density constraints

The theorem is stated using one quantum or polynomial constraint $\mathcal Q_p=0$. To express it using only ordinary density prescriptions, expand

```math
Q_p(x,y)
=
\sum_{a+b\leq2q}c_{a,b}x^ay^b.
```

It is sufficient to prescribe the finitely many bi-star moments

```math
\int x^ay^b\,d\nu
```

appearing with nonzero coefficients to their target values. Replacing every bi-star homomorphism density by the finite sum of its induced completions produces an ordinary finite forcing family.

The forcing order grows linearly with the number of layers. This is compatible with `findings-60.md`: each fixed $q$-layer target is finitely selectable, but no single bounded-order list can identify the ordered-layer sectors for unbounded $q$, much less the entire central boundary.

# 7. Structural meaning

The selector has two logically distinct parts. The zero densities of $A$ and $P$ force the qualitative relational law: incomparability becomes equality of layer, and the quotient becomes a linear order. The polynomial $Q_p$ then fixes the quantitative masses of those layers through graph-intrinsic rooted degree data.

No latent layer labels occur in the constraints. The selected partition is reconstructed from the relation itself.

For rational layer vectors, exact expansion of $Q_p$ agrees with its bi-star moment representation. Enumeration of rational strict weak-order competitors gives zero forcing energy only at the prescribed ordered-layer sector.

# 8. Literature relation

Finite forcibility asks whether finitely many substructure densities determine a unique limit object. Lovász and Szegedy developed this framework for graphons and recalled the Lovász-Sós theorem that every finite step graphon is finitely forcible. The theorem above is a direct DAG-boundary result specialized to deterministic ordered layers; its proof uses strict weak-order structure and does not assume a general directed analogue of the Lovász-Sós theorem.

Reference:

- L. Lovász and B. Szegedy, *Finitely forcible graphons*, Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B 101 (2011), 269-301, arXiv:0901.0929.
