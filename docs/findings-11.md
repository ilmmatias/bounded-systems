# 1. The reachability poset

Define

$$
u\le v
$$

when either $u=v$ or there is a positive-length directed path from $u$ to $v$.

A5 implies:

* reflexivity after adjoining equality;
* antisymmetry;
* transitivity.

Therefore

$$
P_G=(V,\le)
$$

is a partially ordered set.

The original edge relation need not equal the order relation:

$$
u\to v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
u<v,
$$

but $u<v$ may hold only through a longer route.

# 2. The thin reachability category

Construct a category $\mathsf R(G)$ with:

* objects $v\in V$;
* exactly one morphism
$$
  [v,u]:u\to v
$$
  whenever $u\le v$;
* no morphism otherwise.

Composition is forced:

$$
[w,v][v,u]=[w,u].
$$

It is called thin because every ordered pair has at most one morphism.

There is a canonical functor

$$
q:\mathsf P(G)\longrightarrow\mathsf R(G)
$$

which:

* fixes every node;
* sends every finite path $p:u\to v$ to the unique morphism $[v,u]$.

Thus all routes with the same endpoints are identified:

$$
p\sim q
\iff
s(p)=s(q),\qquad r(p)=r(q).
$$

## Universal property

Suppose a functor $F$ out of the path category treats every two paths with the same endpoints as equal. Then $F$ factors uniquely through $\mathsf R(G)$:

$$
\mathsf P(G)
\xrightarrow{q}
\mathsf R(G)
\xrightarrow{\widetilde F}
\mathsf C.
$$

So $\mathsf R(G)$ is exactly the universal route-insensitive image of the system.

# 3. Algebraic reachability quotient

Let

$$
\mathcal A(G)=\mathbb K[G]
$$

be the algebraic path algebra.

Define the finite-support reachability algebra

$$
\mathcal C_c(P_G)=
\bigoplus_{u\le v}\mathbb K\varepsilon_{v,u},
$$

with multiplication

$$
\varepsilon_{w,v}\varepsilon_{v,u}=
\varepsilon_{w,u},
$$

and zero when the middle nodes do not match.

This algebra exists for every set-sized poset. It requires no local finiteness.

Define

$$
Q:\mathcal A(G)\longrightarrow\mathcal C_c(P_G)
$$

by

$$
Q(p)=\varepsilon_{r(p),s(p)}.
$$

Then $Q$ is a surjective algebra homomorphism.

Its kernel is

$$
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{route}}=
\left\{
\sum_pc_pp:
\sum_{\substack{p:s(p)=u\\r(p)=v}}c_p=0
\text{ for every }u,v
\right\}.
$$

Equivalently, it is generated by all differences

$$
p-q
$$

where $p$ and $q$ have the same source and target.

Hence there is an exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{route}}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal A(G)
\xrightarrow{Q}
\mathcal C_c(P_G)
\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathcal A(G)/\mathcal I_{\mathrm{route}}
\cong
\mathcal C_c(P_G).
$$

The kernel is precisely the algebraic information distinguishing alternative routes.

# 4. What the quotient forgets

The reachability algebra retains:

* the nodes;
* the order $u\le v$;
* which pairs can communicate indirectly;
* composition of comparabilities.

It loses:

* parallel primitive channels;
* whether communication is direct or indirect;
* the number of routes;
* path lengths;
* intermediate nodes;
* branching and reconvergence;
* shortcut edges;
* the distinction between two paths with the same endpoints.

For example,

$$
u\to v
$$

and

$$
u\to w\to v
$$

both map to

$$
\varepsilon_{v,u}.
$$

If both exist, their difference lies in $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{route}}$.

# 5. Endpoint route-defect spaces

For a fixed ordered pair $u\le v$, consider the corner

$$
e_v\mathcal A(G)e_u.
$$

It has as basis all paths from $u$ to $v$.

The quotient map restricts to

$$
e_v\mathcal A e_u
\longrightarrow
\mathbb K\varepsilon_{v,u},
$$

sending

$$
\sum_{p:u\to v}c_pp
\longmapsto
\left(\sum_{p:u\to v}c_p\right)\varepsilon_{v,u}.
$$

Its kernel is

$$
\mathcal K_{v,u}=
e_v\mathcal I_{\mathrm{route}}e_u.
$$

If there are $m<\infty$ paths from $u$ to $v$, then

$$
\dim \mathcal K_{v,u}=m-1.
$$

Thus the family

$$
{\mathcal K_{v,u}}_{u\le v}
$$

measures exactly how much route information is discarded at each comparable pair.

# 6. The original grading usually does not descend

The path algebra is graded by original path length:

$$
\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal A_n.
$$

But the quotient may identify paths of different lengths.

If the system contains both

$$
u\to v
$$

and

$$
u\to w\to v,
$$

then a degree-one path and a degree-two path both become

$$
\varepsilon_{v,u}.
$$

Therefore the original length grading generally does not survive in

$$
\mathcal C_c(P_G).
$$

The quotient instead has its own intrinsic filtration by factorability.

Let

$$
N
=
\mathrm{span}\{
\varepsilon_{v,u}:u<v
\}.
$$

Then

$$
N^n
$$

is generated by comparabilities that can be factored through a strict chain of at least $n$ steps.

This concerns chains in the reachability order, not original channel lengths.

# 7. Primitive elements become cover relations

In the full path algebra,

$$
J/J^2
$$

recovers the original primitive channels.

In the reachability algebra,

$$
N/N^2
$$

has a different meaning.

A strict comparable pair $u<v$ survives in $N/N^2$ exactly when no node lies strictly between them:

$$
\nexists w\quad
u<w<v.
$$

Such a pair is a **cover relation**, written

$$
u\lessdot v.
$$

Therefore

$$
N/N^2=
\text{linear span of the Hasse-cover relations}.
$$

This need not agree with the original edge set.

Two losses occur:

1. parallel original edges become one relation;
2. an original shortcut edge $u\to v$ disappears from $N/N^2$ whenever
$$
   u<w<v
$$
   for some $w$.

So the reachability quotient reconstructs the Hasse diagram of the order, not the original communication channels.

# 8. Dense orders expose the loss sharply

Consider a reachability order in which every comparable pair has an intermediate node:

$$
u<v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists w,\quad u<w<v.
$$

Then there are no cover relations.

Algebraically,

$$
N=N^2,
$$

and therefore

$$
N/N^2=0.
$$

Yet the reachability order may be highly nontrivial.

For example, a system whose reachability order is modeled on the rational order can have communication between many pairs while possessing no Hasse edges at all.

This proves:

$$
\text{the thin reachability category need not be freely generated by primitive covers}.
$$

The full path category always has primitive edge generators. Its thin quotient may not.

# 9. Local nilpotence survives

Even for an arbitrary infinite poset, the strict-order ideal $N$ in the finite-support algebra is locally nilpotent.

Take finitely many elements of $N$. Their supports involve only finitely many nodes. A sufficiently long nonzero product would require a strictly increasing chain that repeats a node, which antisymmetry forbids.

Thus

$$
N\text{ is locally nilpotent}.
$$

As before,

$$
N\text{ is globally nilpotent}
$$

exactly when the poset has a uniform finite bound on strict-chain length.

# 10. The incidence algebra

The finite-support algebra always exists. A much larger completion is available when the poset is **locally finite**.

A poset is locally finite when every closed interval

$$
[u,v]=
\{w:u\le w\le v\}
$$

is finite.

Define the incidence algebra

$$
\mathcal I(P_G)=
\left\{
f:{(u,v):u\le v}\to\mathbb K
\right\}.
$$

Multiplication is convolution:

$$
(f*g)(u,v)=
\sum_{u\le w\le v}
f(u,w)g(w,v).
$$

Local finiteness ensures that every such sum is finite.

The identity is

$$
\delta(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
1,&u=v,\\
0,&u\ne v.
\end{cases}
$$

The finite-support reachability algebra embeds naturally into $\mathcal I(P_G)$.

Thus:

$$
\mathcal C_c(P_G)
\subseteq
\mathcal I(P_G)
$$

when $P_G$ is locally finite.

# 11. Local finiteness is not supplied by A1–A5

A1–A5 do not imply that intervals are finite.

For example, take nodes

$$
u,\quad v,\quad {w_i:i\in I}
$$

with channels

$$
u\to w_i\to v
$$

for every $i\in I$, where $I$ is infinite.

Then

$$
[u,v]
$$

contains every $w_i$, so it is infinite.

The system is nevertheless:

* acyclic;
* of finite height two;
* free of infinite forward paths;
* free of infinite backward paths.

Therefore none of the following implies local finiteness:

* acyclicity;
* finite height;
* existence of both ordinal ranks;
* absence of infinite boundaries.

Conversely, a poset may be locally finite and still contain an infinite chain, such as

$$
0<1<2<3<\cdots.
$$

Local finiteness and boundary behavior are independent properties.

# 12. Why arbitrary incidence convolution fails without it

Suppose an interval $[u,v]$ contains infinitely many intermediate nodes.

For arbitrary functions $f$ and $g$, the proposed convolution contains

$$
\sum_{u\le w\le v}
f(u,w)g(w,v),
$$

which may be an arbitrary infinite scalar sum.

A1–A5 provide no:

* topology on $\mathbb K$;
* summability convention;
* measure;
* ordering of summation;
* convergence rule.

So there is no canonical multiplication on all interval functions.

Without local finiteness, the options are restricted constructions such as:

* the finite-support algebra $\mathcal C_c(P_G)$;
* a finitary incidence algebra with support conditions;
* an analytically summable incidence algebra after choosing a norm;
* convolution over another complete semiring.

Only the first is completely unconditional.

Therefore:

$$
\text{the full incidence algebra is conditional on interval finiteness or another summation structure}.
$$

# 13. The zeta element

For a locally finite reachability poset, define

$$
\zeta(u,v)=1
\qquad(u\le v).
$$

This is the incidence-algebra element recording reachability itself.

Write

$$
\zeta=\delta+\eta,
$$

where

$$
\eta(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
1,&u<v,\\
0,&u=v.
\end{cases}
$$

For a fixed interval $[u,v]$,

$$
\eta^{*n}(u,v)
$$

counts strict chains

$$
u=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n=v.
$$

Because $[u,v]$ is finite, only finitely many chain lengths occur.

Hence the formal geometric series terminates interval by interval:

$$
\zeta^{-1}=
\delta-\eta+\eta^{*2}-\eta^{*3}+\cdots.
$$

The inverse is the Möbius function

$$
\mu=\zeta^{-1}.
$$

# 14. Möbius recursion

The Möbius function satisfies

$$
\mu(u,u)=1
$$

and, for $u<v$,

$$
\mu(u,v)=
-\sum_{u\le w<v}\mu(u,w).
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\mu(u,v)=
-\sum_{u<w\le v}\mu(w,v).
$$

It also has the alternating-chain expression

$$
\mu(u,v)=
\sum_{n\ge0}
(-1)^n
c_n(u,v),
$$

where $c_n(u,v)$ is the number of strict $n$-step chains from $u$ to $v$.

This is finite interval by interval under local finiteness.

# 15. Möbius inversion

Suppose functions $f,g:V\to\mathbb K$ satisfy

$$
g(v)=
\sum_{u\le v}f(u),
$$

with the sums finite or otherwise well-defined.

Then

$$
f(v)=
\sum_{u\le v}\mu(u,v)g(u).
$$

This reverses cumulative aggregation over the reachability order.

It is an inversion of order-based accumulation, not an inversion of the original path structure.

# 16. What Möbius inversion cannot recover

Möbius inversion knows only the poset $P_G$.

It cannot reconstruct:

* how many original edges connect $u$ and $v$;
* whether $u<v$ arose through one route or many;
* whether there was a shortcut edge;
* original path lengths;
* parallel channels;
* route interference.

Two systems with the same transitive closure have the same incidence algebra and the same Möbius function, even if their channel structures are very different.

For instance,

$$
u\to w\to v
$$

and

$$
u\to w\to v,\qquad u\to v
$$

have the same reachability poset.

Their path algebras differ, but their incidence algebras coincide.

So:

$$
\mu\text{ inverts reachability aggregation, not channel composition}.
$$

# 17. The formal path completion does not automatically descend

Recall the formal path completion

$$
\widehat{\mathcal A}(G)=
\mathbb K^{\mathsf P(G)}.
$$

At the algebraic finite-support level, $Q$ sums the coefficients of all paths with the same endpoints.

For a formal element $X$, this would require

$$
Q(X)(u,v)=
\sum_{p:u\to v}X(p).
$$

That sum may be infinite.

Even a fixed path length may contain infinitely many routes from $u$ to $v$. For example,

$$
u\to w_i\to v
$$

for infinitely many $i$ gives infinitely many length-two paths.

Therefore the quotient map

$$
Q:\mathcal A(G)\to\mathcal C_c(P_G)
$$

does **not** automatically extend to

$$
\widehat{\mathcal A}(G).
$$

In particular, naively sending the full formal resolvent

$$
R_G(z)=\sum_pz^{|p|}p
$$

to a scalar incidence function would require summing all route multiplicities within each endpoint pair.

That may be undefined.

This gives a noncommuting diagram:

$$
\text{complete all paths first}
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{a canonical completed reachability quotient}.
$$

Extra summability conditions are needed.

# 18. Support descends even when coefficients do not

Although coefficients may not be summable, the existence statement always descends:

$$
u\le v
\iff
\exists p:u\to v.
$$

Thus the support of the full path family determines the zeta relation:

$$
\zeta(u,v)=
\begin{cases}
1,&\text{some finite path }u\to v\text{ exists},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
$$

This is an existence projection rather than a linear coefficient sum:

$$
\text{many routes}\longmapsto \text{true}.
$$

So there are two fundamentally different quotient semantics:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{linear quotient:}&\quad
\text{add coefficients of parallel routes},\\
\text{support quotient:}&\quad
\text{retain only whether at least one route exists}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The second always exists; the first may require finiteness or convergence.

# 19. Rank-filtered incidence blocks

When the reachability poset is locally finite, the ordinal projections also filter the zeta and Möbius elements.

For past-rank layers, define

$$
\zeta^-_{\beta,\alpha}=
\Delta^-_\beta\zeta\Delta^-_\alpha.
$$

This block is supported only when

$$
\alpha\le\beta.
$$

Likewise,

$$
\mu^-_{\beta,\alpha}=
\Delta^-_\beta\mu\Delta^-_\alpha
$$

has the same triangular support.

Across a past-rank cut $P+Q=1$,

$$
\zeta=
\begin{pmatrix}
\zeta_P&0\\
B&\zeta_Q
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Its inverse is

$$
\mu=
\begin{pmatrix}
\mu_P&0\\
-\mu_QB\mu_P&\mu_Q
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Thus the cross-rank Möbius coefficients are obtained by subtracting all factorizations through lower and higher sectors.

This is the order-theoretic analogue of the rank-filtered resolvent factorization, but it is much less route-sensitive.

# 20. The resulting hierarchy

We now have four increasingly compressed levels:

$$
\begin{array}{ccl}
\text{Full path category}
&:&
\text{every primitive channel and every route},[1mm]
\text{Path algebra}
&:&
\text{linear combinations and noncommutative composition},[1mm]
\text{Reachability category/algebra}
&:&
\text{one morphism per comparable pair},[1mm]
\text{Incidence algebra}
&:&
\text{scalar convolution over intervals}.
\end{array}
$$

The information loss is:

$$
\text{routes}
\to
\text{endpoint comparability}
\to
\text{interval aggregation}.
$$

The full bounded-system object remains

$$
(\mathcal A(G),\mathcal D(G)),
$$

while the reachability and incidence constructions are derived quotients.
