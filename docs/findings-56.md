# 1. Extremal central sectors beyond finite layers

The preceding three findings established exact support decompositions and process limits for statistics generated by independent vertex variables and independent pair variables. The finite ordered-layer sectors supplied explicit examples, but finiteness of the layer alphabet was not used in the structural proofs. The essential assumptions were instead square integrability, dissociation, and the representation of every finite observation as a measurable function of finitely many independent vertex and pair coordinates.

The finite-layer restriction is unnecessary. Every extremal central sector of the simple-DAG branching graph has the required representation, so the complete finite-pattern continuum theorem extends to the entire dense exchangeable extremal boundary.

Let $\mathbb P$ be an extremal coherent measure on the DAG branching graph of `findings-47.md`, and let $G_\infty$ be the corresponding exchangeable infinite labeled DAG. For each unordered pair $\{i,j\}$, record the joint directed-edge state

```math
Z_{ij}=(A_{ij},A_{ji})\in\{0,1\}^2,
```

where $A_{ij}=1$ means that the edge $i\to j$ is present. In a simple DAG, the state $(1,1)$ has probability zero, and the resulting infinite array has no finite directed cycle almost surely.

# 2. Dissociated Aldous-Hoover representation

Extremality implies dissociation: restrictions of $G_\infty$ to disjoint finite vertex sets are independent. The Aldous-Hoover representation for jointly exchangeable arrays therefore has no global mixing variable.

## Theorem 1. Pair-state representation of an extremal sector

There exist independent random variables

```math
(U_i)_{i\geq1},
\qquad
(V_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty},
```

all uniformly distributed on $[0,1]$, and a measurable function

```math
\Phi:[0,1]^3\longrightarrow\{0,1\}^2
```

such that

```math
(A_{ij},A_{ji})
=
\Phi(U_i,U_j,V_{ij}),
\qquad i<j,
```

and

```math
\Phi(y,x,v)
=
\operatorname{swap}\Phi(x,y,v)
```

almost everywhere. The function $\Phi$ may be chosen so that the sampled array is acyclic almost surely.

Equivalently, after integrating out $V_{ij}$, the law is represented by a deterministic digraphon

```math
W=(W_{00},W_{01},W_{10},W_{11}),
```

where $W_{ab}(x,y)$ is the conditional probability of the pair state $(a,b)$ given $(U_i,U_j)=(x,y)$.

## Proof

The central-exchangeable correspondence of `findings-47.md` gives an exchangeable infinite directed graph. Extremality gives the dissociation property proved there. The jointly exchangeable-array representation theorem then supplies a measurable representation

```math
(A_{ij},A_{ji})
=
\Phi(U_0,U_i,U_j,V_{ij}).
```

For an extreme law, the global variable $U_0$ can be omitted. The symmetry relation follows by storing both directed edge indicators in one unordered-pair state. Since the represented array has the same law as $G_\infty$, the DAG constraint is inherited almost surely.

The last statement follows by setting

```math
W_{ab}(x,y)
=
\lambda
\left\{
 v\in[0,1]:\Phi(x,y,v)=(a,b)
\right\},
```

where $\lambda$ is Lebesgue measure.

The representation is not unique. Measure-preserving changes of the vertex coordinate and redundant auxiliary randomness may alter $\Phi$ without altering the exchangeable DAG law.

# 3. Finite induced patterns as generalized U-statistics

Let $F$ be an unlabeled finite DAG with $k$ vertices. Define the symmetric bounded kernel

```math
f_F
\left(
 (u_a)_{a\in[k]},
 (v_{ab})_{1\leq a<b\leq k}
\right)
```

to be the indicator that the directed pair states generated by $\Phi$ form a DAG isomorphic to $F$. The induced density of $F$ in the restriction $G_n=G_\infty|_{[n]}$ is then

```math
T_n(F)
=
\frac1{(n)_k}
\sum_{\phi:[k]\hookrightarrow[n]}
(f_F)_\phi.
```

Its expectation is the sector coordinate

```math
p_F
=
\mathbb P(G_\infty|_{[k]}\cong F).
```

Every finite induced-pattern observable is therefore a bounded generalized U-statistic of the joint vertex-pair form treated in `findings-53.md`-`findings-55.md`. No finite partition of the latent space is required.

# 4. Intrinsic stable coefficient hierarchy

Choose an Aldous-Hoover representation and let $(f_F)_P$ denote the canonical projection of $f_F$ onto a looped support pattern $P$. For finite DAGs $F$ and $K$ with $k$ and $\ell$ vertices, define

```math
C_r(F,K)
=
\sum_{\substack{P\\v(P)=r}}
\frac{(k)_r(\ell)_r}{|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|}
\mathbb E
\left[
(f_F)_P(f_K)_P
\right].
```

The sum is finite because only support patterns on at most $\min(k,\ell)$ vertices occur.

## Theorem 2. Universal exact covariance expansion

For all $n\leq m$ large enough to contain both patterns,

```math
\operatorname{Cov}
\left(
T_n(F),T_m(K)
\right)
=
\sum_{r=1}^{\min(k,\ell)}
\frac{C_r(F,K)}{(m)_r}.
```

The aggregated coefficients $C_r(F,K)$ are independent of the chosen Aldous-Hoover representation.

## Proof

The exact identity is Theorem 3 of `findings-53.md` applied to the bounded kernels $f_F$ and $f_K$.

For representation independence, the left-hand side is determined entirely by the exchangeable DAG law. The functions

```math
m\longmapsto\frac1{(m)_r},
\qquad
1\leq r\leq\min(k,\ell),
```

are linearly independent on the integers $m\geq\max(k,\ell)$. Hence the coefficients in the finite expansion are unique. A different Aldous-Hoover representation may split an order-$r$ coefficient among different looped support patterns, but it cannot change their sum.

The coefficient hierarchy can therefore be recovered without access to latent variables. Recursively,

```math
C_1(F,K)
=
\lim_{m\to\infty}
 m\,
\operatorname{Cov}(T_m(F),T_m(K)),
```

and, after $C_1,\ldots,C_{r-1}$ have been determined,

```math
C_r(F,K)
=
\lim_{m\to\infty}
(m)_r
\left[
\operatorname{Cov}(T_m(F),T_m(K))
-
\sum_{j<r}
\frac{C_j(F,K)}{(m)_j}
\right].
```

For $F=K$, every $C_r(F,F)$ is nonnegative.

# 5. Intrinsic principal degree

The principal degree of a nonconstant finite pattern observable is

```math
d(F)
=
\min
\left\{
 r\geq1:C_r(F,F)>0
\right\}.
```

This integer is intrinsic. It can be read from the decay of the finite-level variance, without selecting latent coordinates:

```math
\operatorname{Var}(T_n(F))
\sim
\frac{C_{d(F)}(F,F)}{n^{d(F)}}.
```

The individual canonical support patterns producing this coefficient may depend on the representation. The normalization exponent, the stable covariance coefficient, and the law of the full observable do not.

# 6. Universal finite-pattern process limit

Set

```math
m_N(s)=\lfloor Ne^s\rfloor.
```

## Theorem 3. Continuum process in an arbitrary extremal sector

For every finite DAG pattern $F$,

```math
Z_{F,N}(s)
=
 m_N(s)^{d(F)/2}
\left(
T_{m_N(s)}(F)-p_F
\right)
```

converges on compact logarithmic-time intervals to a stationary process $Z_F$. In any Aldous-Hoover representation, $Z_F$ is the finite sum of the principal support-pattern Wick fields

```math
Z_F(s)
=
\sum_{\substack{P\\v(P)=d(F)}}
\frac{(k)_{d(F)}}{|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|}
\mathbb V_P(s;(f_F)_P).
```

Its covariance is intrinsic and equals

```math
\mathbb E[Z_F(s)Z_K(t)]
=
C_d(F,K)e^{-d|s-t|/2}
```

whenever $d(F)=d(K)=d$. The result holds jointly for every finite collection of patterns.

If all principal support patterns in one representation are connected, the limit is Gaussian and is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with rate $d/2$. In general, it is a stationary Wick polynomial in connected Ornstein-Uhlenbeck fields of total support degree $d$.

## Proof

The finite pattern kernel is bounded and therefore square-integrable. The exact decomposition of `findings-53.md`, the connected invariance principle of `findings-54.md`, and the disconnected Wick theorem of `findings-55.md` apply directly. Higher support orders vanish after multiplication by $m_N^{d/2}$. The covariance identity follows from Theorem 2 or from the Wick isometry.

Because the prelimit statistic is graph-intrinsic, the weak law of the sum on the right is the same for every valid representation, even though the latent-field realization of that law need not be unique.

# 7. Finite-pattern continuum limits

Within the classical central boundary of the simple-DAG branching graph, every extremal sector and every finite induced-pattern observable has a deterministic pattern limit, an exact finite-level coefficient expansion, an intrinsic principal normalization, a functional logarithmic-size limit, and relaxation rate $d/2$.

Thus stable coefficients and finite-pattern continuum limits do not require finite latent dimension. They are universal consequences of extremal exchangeability and the vertex-pair representation.

This does not yet prove a closed evolution on a finite-dimensional macroscopic state. The complete coordinate family

```math
\left(T_n(F)\right)_{F\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{fin}}}
```

is countably infinite, and different sectors may require infinitely many pattern coordinates for identification. Nor does the theorem select one extremal sector from the central boundary. It instead proves that every selected extremal sector possesses a complete finite-pattern fluctuation theory.

The result also remains confined to the dense central regime. As shown in `findings-47.md`, a nonempty exchangeable DAG on a fixed countable vertex set has positive edge density. Sparse nonempty limits require a different symmetry or sampling framework.

# 8. Role of acyclicity

The support decomposition itself is a theorem about dissociated exchangeable pair-state arrays. Acyclicity restricts the admissible kernels $\Phi$ or digraphons $W$; it does not alter the projection mechanism once a valid sector has been chosen.

For a DAG sector, every finite directed-cycle density vanishes. This places nonlinear constraints on $W$, and it may permit additional structure such as measurable order coordinates. The universal process theorem does not require a classification of those constraints. It uses only that the sampled finite pair states are almost surely DAGs.

A separate classification problem remains:

```math
\text{Which dissociated digraphons generate DAGs almost surely?}
```

Solving that problem would describe the full extremal central boundary in kernel coordinates. The present theorem applies before that classification is available.

# 9. Literature relation

The pair-state representation is the directed specialization of the Aldous-Hoover theorem. The directed-graph version is formulated explicitly in P. Diaconis and S. Janson, *Graph limits and exchangeable random graphs*, Rendiconti di Matematica 28 (2008), 33-61, arXiv:0712.2749, which shows that extreme exchangeable directed-graph laws admit a representation without a global variable. D. Cai, N. Ackerman, and C. Freer, *Priors on exchangeable directed graphs*, Electronic Journal of Statistics 10 (2016), 1112-1145, arXiv:1510.08440, package the same structure as a digraphon with four joint edge-direction probabilities.

The generalized U-statistic form generated by independent vertex and pair variables is studied in S. Janson and K. Nowicki, *The asymptotic distributions of generalized U-statistics with applications to random graphs*, Probability Theory and Related Fields 90 (1991), 341-375. Their projection terms are indexed by support graphs; the smallest support order determines the normalization, and connected principal supports give Gaussian limits. The support-pattern results above add the exact nested-level covariance and logarithmic process form needed here.
