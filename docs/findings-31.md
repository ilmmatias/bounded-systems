# 1. The universal fiber-collapse construction

Let

$$
\pi:X\longrightarrow Y
$$

be any map of sets.

For $y\in Y$, write

$$
X_y=\pi^{-1}(y).
$$

Define the algebraic pushforward

$$
\Sigma_\pi^0:k^{(X)}\longrightarrow k^{(Y)}
$$

by

$$
(\Sigma_\pi^0f)(y)=
\sum_{x\in X_y}f(x).
$$

The sum is always finite because $f$ has finite support.

Its kernel is

$$
\mathfrak D_\pi=
\ker\Sigma_\pi^0=
\bigoplus_{y\in Y}
\left\lbrace f\in k^{(X_y)}: \sum_{x\in X_y}f(x)=0 \right\rbrace.
$$

Thus we have the exact sequence

$$
0\longrightarrow
\mathfrak D_\pi
\longrightarrow
k^{(X)}
\xrightarrow{\Sigma_\pi^0}
k^{(\pi(X))}
\longrightarrow0.
$$

Every defect is generated by differences

$$
\delta_x-\delta_{x'}
\qquad
\bigl(\pi(x)=\pi(x')\bigr).
$$

So this one construction already contains the general form of every route-collapse ideal we have encountered.

# 2. The kernel-pair groupoid

Associated to $\pi$ is the equivalence-relation groupoid

$$
\mathcal G_\pi=
X\times_YX=
\{(x,x'):\pi(x)=\pi(x')\}.
$$

Its operations are

$$
(x,x')^{-1}=(x',x),
$$

and

$$
(x,x')(x',x'')=(x,x'').
$$

The unit at $x$ is $(x,x)$.

Inside one fiber $X_y$, this is the full pair groupoid

$$
X_y\times X_y.
$$

Its algebraic matrix units act on $k^{(X)}$ by

$$
E_{x,x'}\delta_{x'}=\delta_x.
$$

Then

$$
(E_{x,x'}-E_{x',x'})\delta_{x'}=
\delta_x-\delta_{x'}.
$$

The groupoid records both which elements collapse and every coherent replacement operation within each collapse fiber.

For a discrete equivalence relation, the regular groupoid representation gives matrix blocks over its equivalence classes; under locally compact Hausdorff étale hypotheses, the usual convolution algebra and full/reduced groupoid $C^\ast$-completions become available.

# 3. Hilbert-space regularity of a general collapse

On algebraic finite-support vectors, define

$$
\Sigma_\pi^0\delta_x=\delta_{\pi(x)}
$$

as an operator

$$
\ell^2(X)\supset k^{(X)}
\longrightarrow
\ell^2(Y).
$$

Exactly the same trichotomy derived for endpoint collapse holds.

## Uniformly finite fibers

If

$$
M=\sup_{y\in Y}|X_y|<\infty,
$$

then $\Sigma_\pi^0$ is bounded and

$$
|\Sigma_\pi|=\sqrt M.
$$

## Individually finite but unbounded fibers

If every $X_y$ is finite but their sizes are unbounded, then $\Sigma_\pi^0$ is closable and its closure is unbounded.

## An infinite fiber

If some $X_y$ is infinite, then $\Sigma_\pi^0$ is nonclosable.

Indeed, choosing distinct $x_1,\ldots,x_N\in X_y$,

$$
f_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N\delta_{x_j}
$$

satisfies

$$
|f_N|_2=N^{-1/2}\to0
$$

but

$$
\Sigma_\pi^0f_N=\delta_y.
$$

Thus the analytic behavior depends only on the collapse-fiber cardinalities.

This will apply simultaneously to finite route fibers and Martin-boundary fibers.

# 4. Finite path collapse as a kernel pair

Take

$$
X_{\mathrm{fin}}=\mathsf P(G)
$$

and

$$
Y_{\mathrm{fin}}=
\{(u,v):u\le v\}.
$$

Define

$$
\pi_{\mathrm{fin}}(p)=
\bigl(s(p),r(p)\bigr).
$$

Then

$$
\Sigma_{\pi_{\mathrm{fin}}}^0(p)=
\varepsilon_{r(p),s(p)},
$$

the unique thin reachability arrow with the same endpoints.

After linearization, this is exactly

$$
Q:A(G)\longrightarrow B(P_G).
$$

Consequently,

$$
\mathfrak D_{\pi_{\mathrm{fin}}}=
\ker Q=
I.
$$

The finite kernel-pair groupoid is

$$
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{fin}}=
\{
(p,q):
s(p)=s(q),;
r(p)=r(q)
\}.
$$

Its arrows are replacements of one actual route by another parallel route.

This exposes two independent compositions:

### Horizontal composition

Ordinary path concatenation:

$$
q p.
$$

### Vertical composition

Route replacement:

$$
(p,q)(q,r)=(p,r).
$$

They are not the same multiplication.

This is the first genuinely two-directional structure produced by the system:

$$
\text{horizontal: communication composition},
\qquad
\text{vertical: route identification}.
$$

# 5. Finite route replacements propagate to infinite histories

Let $p,q:u\to v$ be finite paths with the same endpoints.

Let

$$
\zeta=v\to\cdots
$$

be an infinite tail beginning at $v$.

Then

$$
p\zeta
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
q\zeta
$$

are two infinite histories beginning at $u$.

They eventually have exactly the same tail, so they define the same node Martin cluster set and, when regular, the same node Martin point.

Thus every finite route replacement produces a boundary replacement:

$$
(p,q;\zeta)
\longmapsto
(p\zeta,q\zeta).
$$

In the standard graph boundary-path groupoid, sets of the form

$$
Z(p,q)=
\{
(p\zeta,\ |p|-|q|,\ q\zeta)
\}
$$

are the basic prefix-replacement bisections. Their multiplication and inversion encode composition of finite prefix replacements.

# 6. Boundary creator representation

Let

$$
\mathcal H_\partial=
\ell^2(X_{\mathrm{pre}})
$$

be the Hilbert space with one orthogonal basis vector for each boundary history in the chosen prefix-boundary space.

A finite path $p$ defines a partial creator

$$
L_p^\partial\delta_\zeta=
\delta_{p\zeta}
$$

when $\zeta$ begins at $r(p)$.

For parallel paths $p,q$, define

$$
U_{p,q}=
L_p^\partial(L_q^\partial)^\ast.
$$

It acts by

$$
U_{p,q}\delta_{q\zeta}=
\delta_{p\zeta}.
$$

For paths $p,q,r$ with the same terminal node,

$$
U_{p,q}^\ast=U_{q,p},
$$

and

$$
U_{p,q}U_{q,r}=U_{p,r}.
$$

These are matrix-unit relations inside a boundary-tail fiber.

The finite route defect acts as

$$
(L_p^\partial-L_q^\partial)\delta_\zeta=
\delta_{p\zeta}-\delta_{q\zeta}.
$$

Therefore the propagated finite route ideal is exactly the algebraic span of eventual-tail defects:

$$
I,k^{(X_{\mathrm{pre}})}=
\mathrm{span}
\{
\delta_\xi-\delta_\eta:
\xi\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}\eta
\}.
$$

This is the direct finite-to-infinite bridge we were looking for.

# 7. Two nested boundary equivalence relations

Fix a source $u$, a weight $t$, and the set $X_u^{\mathrm{reg}}$ of $t$-Martin-regular histories beginning at $u$.

There are now two equivalence relations.

## Eventual-tail equivalence

$$
\xi\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}\eta
$$

when

$$
\xi=p\zeta,
\qquad
\eta=q\zeta
$$

for some parallel finite routes

$$
p,q:u\to v
$$

and a common infinite tail $\zeta$ beginning at $v$.

This is the equivalence generated by propagated finite route replacements.

## Martin equivalence

$$
\xi\sim_t^M\eta
$$

when

$$
h_\xi=h_\eta.
$$

Because eventual common tails have identical node Martin limits,

$$
\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}
\ \subseteq
\sim_t^M.
$$

Thus there is a two-stage compression:

$$
X_u^{\mathrm{reg}}
\longrightarrow
X_u^{\mathrm{reg}}/\!\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}
\longrightarrow
X_u^{\mathrm{reg}}/\!\sim_t^M.
$$

The first quotient removes finite-prefix route choices.

The second removes distinctions between genuinely different ends that nevertheless have the same normalized Green asymptotics.

# 8. Tail ends inside a Martin fiber

Let

$$
\pi_t:X_u^{\mathrm{reg}}\to Y_{t,u}
$$

be the node Martin map,

$$
\pi_t(\xi)=h_\xi.
$$

For $h\in Y_{t,u}$, define its history fiber

$$
F_h=\pi_t^{-1}(h).
$$

Now quotient this fiber by eventual-tail equivalence:

$$
\overline F_h=
F_h/\!\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}.
$$

An element of $\overline F_h$ is not one history. It is one genuine tail end, with all finite-prefix alternatives already identified.

Therefore:

$$
|\overline F_h|
$$

measures how many distinct tail ends are collapsed into the single node Martin direction $h$.

This separates two kinds of multiplicity that had previously been mixed.

### Prefix multiplicity

Several histories differ only by finite routes and eventually share a tail.

### End multiplicity

Several genuinely different tails have the same Martin shadow.

# 9. Exact defect sequence

Define the algebraic tail-defect module

$$
\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}=
\mathrm{span}
\{
\delta_\xi-\delta_\eta:
\xi\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}\eta
\}.
$$

Define the Martin-defect module

$$
\mathfrak D_M=
\mathrm{span}
\{
\delta_\xi-\delta_\eta:
\xi\sim_t^M\eta
\}.
$$

Then

$$
\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}
\subseteq
\mathfrak D_M.
$$

For a set $S$, write

$$
\widetilde{k}^{(S)}=
\left\lbrace f\in k^{(S)}: \sum_{s\in S}f(s)=0 \right\rbrace.
$$

There is a canonical exact sequence

$$
0
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}
\longrightarrow
\mathfrak D_M
\longrightarrow
\bigoplus_{h\in Y_{t,u}}
\widetilde{k}^{(\overline F_h)}
\longrightarrow
0.
$$

### Why

Inside a Martin fiber $F_h$:

1. $\mathfrak D_M$ permits all zero-total differences among histories in $F_h$;
2. $\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}$ kills differences internal to each tail class;
3. after quotienting, each tail class becomes one basis element;
4. the remaining relation is that the total coefficient over all tail classes is zero.

Thus

$$
\mathfrak D_M/\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}
\cong
\bigoplus_h
\widetilde{k}^{(\overline F_h)}.
$$

This quotient is the **pure asymptotic mixing defect**.

It contains precisely those Martin identifications that cannot be generated by finite route replacements.

---

# 10. Finite-dimensional interpretation

If one Martin point $h$ contains exactly $r$ distinct tail classes, then

$$
\dim
\widetilde{k}^{(\overline F_h)}=
r-1.
$$

So:

* $r=1$: all boundary collapse is explained by finite route ambiguity;
* $r=2$: one independent asymptotic identification between two ends;
* $r>2$: an $(r-1)$-dimensional space of asymptotic end differences.

For infinite $r$, the algebraic module remains well defined, even though a Hilbert collapse to one equal-end direction becomes nonclosable.

---

# 11. Three examples

## Unique-route tree

Every history has its own node Martin point.

Hence

$$
F_h=\{\xi\},
\qquad
\overline F_h=\{\xi\}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathfrak D_M=
\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}=
0.
$$

Nothing is collapsed, either finitely or asymptotically.

---

## Diamond followed by one tail

There are two histories

$$
p\zeta,
\qquad
q\zeta.
$$

They differ by a finite diamond but share the same eventual tail.

Thus

$$
|F_h|=2,
$$

but

$$
|\overline F_h|=1.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathfrak D_M\ne0,
$$

while

$$
\mathfrak D_M/\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}=0.
$$

All boundary multiplicity is inherited from the finite route defect

$$
p-q.
$$

There is no genuinely new asymptotic mixing.

---

## Complete layered mixing

Every infinite symbolic history has the same node Martin point, but two generic histories are not eventually tail-equivalent.

Therefore one Martin fiber contains a vast collection of distinct tail classes:

$$
\overline F_h
\cong
X_{\mathrm{pre}}/\!\sim_{\mathrm{tail}}.
$$

Hence

$$
\widetilde{k}^{(\overline F_h)}
$$

is large.

The Martin collapse here is not produced by any finite diamond or finite reconvergence. It is created by asymptotic mixing of endpoint Green ratios.

---

# 12. The asymptotic mixing nerve

For each Martin point $h$, form the chaotic nerve

$$
E_{\mathrm{mix}}(h)=
E(\overline F_h).
$$

Its simplices are

$$
E_{\mathrm{mix}}(h)_n=
\overline F_h^{,n+1}.
$$

Interpretation:

* vertices: distinct tail ends with Martin shadow $h$;
* edges: asymptotic identifications between two ends;
* triangles: coherence among three such identifications;
* higher simplices: complete higher coherence of the Martin collapse.

Its degree-one boundary is

$$
d_1(\tau_0,\tau_1)=
\tau_1-\tau_0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\mathrm{im}d_1=
\widetilde{k}^{(\overline F_h)}.
$$

So the exact defect sequence can be rewritten as

$$
\text{finite route defects}
\longrightarrow
\text{all Martin defects}
\longrightarrow
\text{asymptotic end-mixing resolution}.
$$

Again the nerve is contractible when nonempty, but its marked vertices retain the distinct ends being collapsed.

# 13. Two-stage boundary resolution

The full collapse of histories inside a Martin fiber can now be resolved in two stages.

## Stage one: finite-prefix collapse

Within each tail class $T\subseteq F_h$, use

$$
E(T).
$$

This resolves all histories related by finite route replacements.

## Stage two: asymptotic-end collapse

Collapse the resulting tail classes using

$$
E(\overline F_h).
$$

Thus the boundary analogue of the earlier route–factorization bicomplex is

$$
\text{history}
\longrightarrow
\text{tail end}
\longrightarrow
\text{Martin direction}.
$$

The first differential is generated by the original finite route ideal $I$.

The second differential records genuinely asymptotic identifications that have no finite route witness.

# 14. Operator-algebraic meaning

The boundary replacement operators

$$
U_{p,q}=L_p^\partial(L_q^\partial)^\ast
$$

generate the algebra of eventual-tail replacements.

This is the operator-algebraic image of the finite kernel-pair groupoid.

The larger Martin relation

$$
\mathcal G_M=
\{
(\xi,\eta):
\xi\sim_t^M\eta
\}
$$

contains that tail groupoid as a subgroupoid.

However, the following restriction remains:

$$
\mathcal G_M
\text{ is not automatically étale or locally compact}.
$$

The standard graph tail groupoid has a canonical topology built from finite-prefix bisections. The Martin relation may have large nondiscrete fibers or may fail to be closed.

Therefore, under A1–A5 alone, the canonical primary object is

$$
\left(
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{tail}}
\subseteq
\mathcal G_M,;
\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}
\subseteq
\mathfrak D_M
\right)
$$

at the set-theoretic and algebraic level.

A standard groupoid convolution algebra or $C^\ast$-completion requires additional topological hypotheses such as local compactness, a suitable Haar system, or étaleness. Standard étale groupoid convolution and completion theory applies once those conditions are present.

---

# 15. Why the algebraic module must remain primary

Suppose a Martin fiber, or even one tail class, is infinite.

Then the algebraic zero-sum defect vectors are dense in its entire $\ell^2$ fiber.

Consequently, taking Hilbert closures too early can erase the distinction between:

* finite-prefix defects;
* asymptotic mixing defects.

The quotient

$$
\overline\{\mathfrak D_M\}/\overline\{\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}\}
$$

can become zero even while

$$
\mathfrak D_M/\mathfrak D_{\mathrm{tail}}
$$

is enormous.

Therefore:

$$
\text{the algebraic defect filtration is more informative than its Hilbert closure}.
$$

This is the boundary version of the earlier fact that infinite route collapse and norm closure do not commute.
