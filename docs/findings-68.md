# 1. Terminal relational potentials

Finite forcing can be inserted directly into the finite-horizon growth construction of `findings-46.md`. The resulting route-counting process selects the forced central sector in the large-horizon, low-temperature limit.

Let $\mu^\star$ be a central sector absolutely forced by finite coordinates $f_1,\ldots,f_m$. For a terminal DAG $K\in\mathfrak D_N$, define

```math
\mathcal E_N(K)
=
\sum_{j=1}^m
\left(
 f_j(K)-f_j(\mu^\star)
\right)^2.
```

Choose an inverse-temperature sequence $\beta_N>0$ and terminal weights

```math
w_N(K)
=
\exp\left(-\beta_N\mathcal E_N(K)\right).
```

The endpoint law generated by weighted path counting is

```math
\pi_N(K)
=
\frac{
 \dim(K)e^{-\beta_N\mathcal E_N(K)}
}{
 \sum_{J\in\mathfrak D_N}
 \dim(J)e^{-\beta_N\mathcal E_N(J)}
}.
```

The dimension factor is the number of labelings of the endpoint class, so this is a Gibbs tilt of uniform labeled-DAG counting by a finite relational energy.

# 2. Existence of low-energy endpoints

Every extremal central sector admits almost-sure finite approximants. If $X$ is sampled from $\mu^\star$ and

```math
K_N=X|_{[N]},
```

then every forcing coordinate converges to its target value. Hence there is a deterministic sequence $K_N^\star$ satisfying

```math
\mathcal E_N(K_N^\star)\longrightarrow0.
```

This is the endpoint approximation theorem of `findings-62.md` applied to the finite forcing family.

# 3. Concentration theorem

## Theorem 1. Zero-temperature selection

Assume

```math
\frac{\beta_N}{N^2}\longrightarrow\infty.
```

Then, for every fixed level $L$ and every $\varepsilon>0$,

```math
\pi_N\left(
K:
\left\|
(T_G(K))_{G\in\mathfrak D_L}
-
\mu_L^\star
\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
>\varepsilon
\right)
\longrightarrow0.
```

## Proof

By Theorem 2 of `findings-67.md`, there are $\delta>0$ and $N_0$ such that every sufficiently large endpoint outside the stated level-$L$ neighborhood has

```math
\mathcal E_N(K)\geq\delta.
```

The total number of labeled simple directed graphs is at most

```math
3^{\binom N2},
```

and the number of labeled DAGs is no larger. Therefore the total unnormalized mass of the bad endpoints is at most

```math
3^{\binom N2}e^{-\beta_N\delta}.
```

The partition function is at least the contribution of $K_N^\star$:

```math
Z_N
\geq
\exp\left(-\beta_N\mathcal E_N(K_N^\star)\right).
```

For large $N$, $\mathcal E_N(K_N^\star)<\delta/2$. The bad probability is consequently bounded by

```math
\exp\left(
\binom N2\log3
-
\frac{\delta}{2}\beta_N
\right),
```

which tends to zero under the assumed scaling.

# 4. Convergence of coherent marginals

The level-$n$ marginal of the finite-horizon path process is the endpoint mixture of uniform bridges:

```math
\mu_n^{N,w}(G)
=
\sum_{K\in\mathfrak D_N}
\pi_N(K)T_G(K).
```

## Corollary 2. Selection of the central profile

For every fixed $n$ and $G\in\mathfrak D_n$,

```math
\mu_n^{N,w}(G)
\longrightarrow
\mu_n^\star(G).
```

The normalized finite-horizon harmonic functions satisfy

```math
\frac{R_{n,N}(G)}{R_{0,N}(\varnothing)}
=
\frac{\mu_n^{N,w}(G)}{\dim(G)}
\longrightarrow
h_n^\star(G).
```

## Proof

Theorem 1 gives convergence in probability of every fixed empirical pattern coordinate under $\pi_N$. Boundedness gives convergence of expectations. The harmonic identity is the finite-horizon form of the profile-to-harmonic reconstruction in `findings-59.md`.

# 5. Convergence of the growth law

## Corollary 3. Selection of the up transition

If $\mu_n^\star(G)>0$, then

```math
U_n^{N,w}(G,H)
\longrightarrow
U_n^{\mu^\star}(G,H)
```

for every $H\in\mathfrak D_{n+1}$, and the convergence holds in total variation over $H$.

## Proof

The finite-horizon transition can be written as

```math
U_n^{N,w}(G,H)
=
D_n(H,G)
\frac{\mu_{n+1}^{N,w}(H)}
     {\mu_n^{N,w}(G)}.
```

Corollary 2 gives convergence of numerator and denominator, and the limiting denominator is positive.

Thus a finite relational terminal potential, together with path multiplicity and canonical deletion, selects both the boundary profile and its forward growth law.

# 6. Ordered-layer selector

Applying the construction to `findings-66.md`, the energy contains only:

- the induced density of the one-edge-plus-isolated pattern;
- the induced density of the open directed path;
- finitely many rooted bi-star moments entering $Q_p$.

No latent layer labels or transition probabilities occur in the terminal weight. In the zero-temperature horizon limit, the selected law is the deterministic $q$-layer central sector with masses $p$.

The result does not claim that this particular potential is forced by the original minimal relational axioms. It proves a sharper conditional statement: once a finite relational selector is specified, the forward stochastic growth law need not be postulated separately. It is reconstructed by finite-horizon route counting and converges to the harmonic law of the selected boundary sector.

# 7. Nonunique minima

If the finite constraints have several central zeros, the same argument concentrates the endpoint measures on their common zero set rather than on one sector. Any remaining mixture appears as the degree-zero boundary mode of `findings-57.md`.

Unique absolute forcing is therefore exactly the condition that removes residual sector uncertainty in this terminal-potential construction.

Enumeration of all labelled DAGs through five vertices gives the exact Gibbs endpoint distribution for increasing inverse temperature in the two-layer forcing model. The induced lower-level bridge marginals and reconstructed up kernels converge toward the minimum-energy sector within this finite model.

# 8. Literature relation

The construction combines finite forcibility with the weighted endpoint and harmonic-transform machinery of the DAG branching graph. Vershik's central-measure framework identifies coherent laws through canonical cotransitions and harmonic functions; the present theorem supplies a finite relational Gibbs mechanism that selects one such harmonic sector.
