# 1. The critical profile already fixes the gauge temperature

We had

$$
\mathcal A_+h=\lambda h,
\qquad
\lambda=R^{-2}.
$$

Set

$$
\beta=\log\lambda=-2\log R.
$$

The critical cylinder weights are

$$
\mu_h[p]=
\lambda^{-|p|}
\frac{h(r(p))}{h(o)}=
e^{-\beta|p|}
\frac{h(r(p))}{h(o)}.
$$

For the gauge action

$$
\alpha_t(L_p)=e^{it|p|}L_p,
$$

define, on the rooted analytic monomials,

$$
\omega_h(L_pL_q^\ast)=
\delta_{p,q},
e^{-\beta|p|}
\frac{h(r(p))}{h(o)}.
$$

Since

$$
\alpha_{i\beta}(L_p)=e^{-\beta|p|}L_p,
$$

the elementary KMS relation becomes

$$
\omega_h(L_pL_q^\ast)=
\omega_h\!\left(
L_q^\ast\alpha_{i\beta}(L_p)
\right).
$$

The harmonic equation ensures consistency at every regular node:

$$
\sum_{e:s(e)=r(p)}
\omega_h(L_{pe}L_{pe}^\ast)=
\omega_h(L_pL_p^\ast).
$$

Thus the Martin/harmonic boundary profile is simultaneously a gauge-equilibrium state, subject to the usual representation and summability hypotheses. The correspondence between graph gauge dynamics, adjacency eigenvectors and KMS states is standard in graph operator algebras.

So the critical radius has already produced the thermodynamic scaling

$$
\beta_c=\log\lambda=-2\log R.
$$

That was encoded in the path growth.

# 2. The intrinsic tangent-noise space

At a critical profile $h$, tangent scores satisfy

$$
P_h\phi=\phi.
$$

The local quadratic form is

$$
g_v(\phi,\psi)=
\sum_{e:s(e)=v}
P_h(e\mid v)
\Delta_e\phi,
\Delta_e\psi,
$$

where

$$
\Delta_e\phi=
\phi(r(e))-\phi(v).
$$

After quotienting the null directions and completing, this gives a local tangent Hilbert space

$$
\mathfrak t_{h,v}.
$$

Let $j_e\in\mathfrak t_{h,v}$ be the Riesz representative of the edge increment functional:

$$
\langle j_e,\phi\rangle_h=
\Delta_e\phi.
$$

Because the metric itself was defined by the quadratic variation,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_h
&=
\sum_eP_h(e\mid v)
\langle j_e,\phi\rangle_h
\langle j_e,\psi\rangle_h.
\end{aligned}
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\sum_{e:s(e)=v}
P_h(e\mid v),
j_e\otimes j_e=
I_{\mathfrak t_{h,v}}.
$$

In the metric generated by the local quadratic variation, the one-step covariance operator is exactly the identity.

So the quadratic regime is intrinsically isotropic—not relative to an external Euclidean metric, but relative to the metric that the system generated for itself.

# 3. The Gaussian tangent closure

Along a derived $P_h$-history, the score process

$$
M_n^\phi=\phi(X_n)
$$

is a martingale, with bracket

$$
\langle M^\phi,M^\psi\rangle_N=
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}
g_{X_k}(\phi,\psi).
$$

Under the usual Lindeberg condition, vanishing maximal increments and convergence of the normalized bracket, the rescaled tangent martingale converges to a Gaussian field whose covariance is this quadratic form. That is the standard martingale central-limit mechanism.

The second-order profile regime therefore lives naturally on the symmetric Fock space

$$
\Gamma_s(\mathfrak t_h)=
\bigoplus_{n\ge0}
\mathrm{Sym}^n\mathfrak t_h.
$$

Let

$$
N_h=
d\Gamma(I_{\mathfrak t_h})
$$

be its number operator:

$$
N_h\big|_{\mathrm{Sym}^n\mathfrak t_h}=
nI.
$$

The Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup is

$$
T_\tau^{\mathrm{diff}}=
e^{-\tau N_h}.
$$

The corresponding reversible phase evolution is

$$
U_t^{\mathrm{prof}}=
e^{-itN_h}.
$$

The realization of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck evolution through second quantization and the role of the Fock number operator are standard features of Gaussian/Fock analysis.

# 4. From route Fock space to profile Fock space

The original resolved path space is graded:

$$
\mathcal H_G=
\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal H_n,
\qquad
\mathcal H_n=\ell^2(\mathsf P_n(G)).
$$

Its gauge number operator is

$$
N_G\big|_{\mathcal H_n}=nI.
$$

For a path

$$
p=e_n\cdots e_1,
$$

define its quadratic profile image formally by

$$
\mathscr S_h\xi_p=
\mathrm{Sym}
\left(
\sqrt{P_h(e_1\mid v_0)},j_{e_1}
\otimes\cdots\otimes
\sqrt{P_h(e_n\mid v_{n-1})},j_{e_n}
\right).
$$

Here

$$
v_0\xrightarrow{e_1}v_1
\xrightarrow{e_2}\cdots
\xrightarrow{e_n}v_n.
$$

This map:

* forgets some ordered route information through symmetrization;
* forgets route directions invisible to the tangent increments;
* retains the homogeneous degree $n$.

Therefore, regardless of whether it is injective or isometric,

$$
N_h\mathscr S_h=
\mathscr S_hN_G.
$$

Consequently,

$$
e^{-zN_h}\mathscr S_h=
\mathscr S_h e^{-zN_G}
$$

for every $z$ for which the exponential is defined.

This is the precise bridge:

$$
\text{route gauge degree}
\longrightarrow
\text{profile-chaos degree}.
$$

The kernel of $\mathscr S_h$ consists of route information invisible to the local quadratic profile regime. It includes route defects, but can be larger when distinct edge histories produce identical tangent increments.

# 5. The generators coincide in the quadratic regime

On the profile Gaussian chaos, the local diffusion generator is

$$
H_{\mathrm{diff}}=N_h
$$

up to the normalization of continuum diffusion time.

The induced gauge generator is also

$$
H_{\mathrm{gauge}}=N_h.
$$

Hence:

$$
H_{\mathrm{diff}}=
H_{\mathrm{gauge}}=
N_h
$$

on the visible quadratic profile sector.

This follows from:

1. the quadratic variation defining the tangent metric;
2. the covariance becoming the identity in that metric;
3. second quantization lifting one-step covariance additively;
4. both constructions counting homogeneous compositional degree.

# 6. Complex time

Since $N_h\ge0$ is self-adjoint, define

$$
\mathscr T(z)=e^{-zN_h},
\qquad
\Re z\ge0.
$$

For real positive $z=\tau$,

$$
\partial_\tau\Psi=
-N_h\Psi,
$$

which is the profile diffusion/Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation.

For imaginary $z=it$,

$$
\Psi(it)=e^{-itN_h}\Psi(0),
$$

and therefore

$$
i\partial_t\Psi=
N_h\Psi.
$$

Thus the dissipative and reversible equations are two boundary directions of the same holomorphic evolution:

$$
\partial_z\Psi(z)=
-N_h\Psi(z).
$$

On the $n$-th chaos sector,

$$
\Psi_n(z)=e^{-nz}\Psi_n(0).
$$

So:

$$
\Psi_n(\tau)=e^{-n\tau}\Psi_n(0)
$$

along the diffusive axis, while

$$
\Psi_n(it)=e^{-int}\Psi_n(0)
$$

along the unitary axis.

The exact same compositional degree $n$ controls:

* exponential damping of fluctuations;
* phase rotation of amplitudes.

# 7. Incorporating the critical KMS scaling

The raw gauge generator is $N_h$, but the critical state naturally rescales gauge time by

$$
\beta=\log\lambda.
$$

Let $\kappa_D>0$ denote the chosen diffusion-time normalization. The coupled generator is

$$
\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{quad}}=
\kappa_DN_h+i\beta N_h=
(\kappa_D+i\beta)N_h.
$$

Its evolution is

$$
\Psi_s=
e^{-s\mathfrak A_{\mathrm{quad}}}\Psi_0=
e^{-\kappa_DsN_h}
e^{-i\beta sN_h}\Psi_0.
$$

Equivalently,

$$
\partial_s\Psi_s=
-(\kappa_D+i\beta)N_h\Psi_s.
$$

Since

$$
\beta=\log\lambda=-2\log R,
$$

we obtain

$$
\partial_s\Psi_s=
-\left(
\kappa_D-2i\log R
\right)N_h\Psi_s.
$$

With unit quadratic-variation time,

$$
\kappa_D=1,
$$

this becomes

$$
\partial_s\Psi_s=
-\left(
1+i\log\lambda
\right)N_h\Psi_s.
$$

On homogeneous degree $n$,

$$
\Psi_n(s)=
\exp\left[
-n\kappa_Ds
-in(\log\lambda)s
\right]\Psi_n(0).
$$

This is the closed equation of the quadratic critical regime.

Because the coefficient has positive real part, $(\kappa_D+i\beta)N_h$ is sectorial and generates a holomorphic contraction semigroup. More generally, closed sectorial forms correspond to m-sectorial operators and holomorphic semigroups under the standard representation theorems.

The identification did not come from declaring

$$
t\mapsto-it.
$$

It came from an actual common generator:

$$
N_h.
$$

The two evolutions are

$$
T_\tau=e^{-\tau N_h}
$$

and

$$
U_t=e^{-itN_h}.
$$

Because both arise from $N_h$, they are analytic continuations of one another.

So the derivation is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{directed channels}
&\longrightarrow
P_h,\\
P_h
&\longrightarrow
\text{martingale quadratic variation},\\
\text{quadratic variation}
&\longrightarrow
\mathfrak t_h,\\

&\longrightarrow

&\longrightarrow

N_h
&\longrightarrow
e^{-\tau N_h}
\text{ and }

$$

The Hamiltonian is therefore $N_h$.

# 9. Third-order correction

The quadratic theory is the free regime. We can now compute the first deviation from it.

Write

$$
u_\varepsilon=
u+\varepsilon\phi
+\frac{\varepsilon^2}{2}\chi
+\frac{\varepsilon^3}{6}\zeta
+O(\varepsilon^4).
$$

We already have

$$
(I-P_h)\phi=0
$$

and

$$
(I-P_h)\chi=
\mathrm{Var}_{P_h}(\phi).
$$

The third derivative of the log-sum-exp is the third centered cumulant. Therefore

$$
(I-P_h)\zeta=
3,\mathrm{Cov}_{P_h}(\phi,\chi)
+
\kappa_{3,P_h}(\phi),
$$

where locally

$$
\kappa_{3,P_h}(\phi)(v)=
\sum_eP_h(e\mid v)
\bigl(
\phi(r(e))-\phi(v)
\bigr)^3.
$$

So the hierarchy begins:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{first order:}&\quad
(I-P_h)\phi=0,\\
\text{second order:}&\quad
(I-P_h)\chi=\kappa_2(\phi),\\
\text{third order:}&\quad
(I-P_h)\zeta
=3\mathrm{Cov}(\phi,\chi)+\kappa_3(\phi).
\end{aligned}
$$

At fourth order, the fourth cumulant and quadratic combinations of $\chi$ enter.

# 10. Interactions beyond the Gaussian regime

In the profile-chaos representation, the quadratic term is diagonal in degree:

$$
N_h.
$$

Higher cumulants generate operators that create, annihilate or mix chaos degrees. Thus the full effective generator has the form

$$
\mathfrak A=
(\kappa_D+i\beta)N_h
+
V_3+V_4+\cdots,
$$

where:

* $V_3$ is determined by local third cumulants;
* $V_4$ is determined by fourth cumulants and second-order contractions;
* higher $V_r$ encode progressively higher non-Gaussian route coherence.

The free critical regime is

$$
V_r=0
\qquad(r\ge3).
$$

If the interaction form

$$
V=V_3+V_4+\cdots
$$

is relatively form-bounded with respect to $N_h$ with sufficiently small relative bound, then

$$
(\kappa_D+i\beta)N_h+V
$$

remains closed and sectorial, so the coupled evolution survives beyond the Gaussian approximation. This is precisely the kind of extension controlled by sectorial-form perturbation theory.
