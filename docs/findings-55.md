# 1. Component factorization of canonical supports

The connected-support theorem gives the primitive Gaussian fields of the continuum limit. A disconnected support pattern does not introduce a new normalization or a new relaxation scale. It combines the connected fields through Wick products.

Let $P$ be a looped support pattern with connected components

```math
P=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_m,
```

and write

```math
r_a=v(C_a),
\qquad
d=v(P)=r_1+\cdots+r_m.
```

The primitive coordinate families belonging to distinct components are independent. If $\mathcal M_P$ denotes the canonical subspace

```math
\mathcal M_P
=
\left\{
g\in L^2(\mathscr F_P):
\mathbb E[g\mid\mathscr F_Q]=0
\text{ for every }Q\subsetneq P
\right\},
```

then the Hilbert-space factorization is

```math
\mathcal M_P
\cong
\widehat\bigotimes_{a=1}^m
\mathcal M_{C_a}.
```

When components are isomorphic, the $P$-symmetric subspace is obtained by symmetrizing the corresponding tensor factors.

## Proposition 1. Canonical tensor factorization

Every $P$-canonical kernel can be approximated in $L^2$ by finite sums of symmetrized tensors

```math
\operatorname{Sym}_P
\left(
\varphi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_m
\right),
qquad
\varphi_a\in\mathcal M_{C_a}.
```

If all primitive coordinate spaces are finite-dimensional, as in a finite-layer Bernoulli ordered-kernel sector, this expansion is finite.

## Proof

The probability space generated by $P$ is the product of the spaces generated by its connected components. Choose orthonormal bases in the one-coordinate spaces consisting of the constant function and centered functions. Their tensor products form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathscr F_P)$.

A tensor basis vector lies in $\mathcal M_P$ exactly when every primitive coordinate appearing in $P$ carries a nonconstant basis factor. Grouping those coordinate factors component by component gives the completed tensor product of the component canonical spaces. Averaging over automorphisms of $P$ gives the symmetric subspace.

# 2. Connected Gaussian fields

For every connected support pattern $C$, the connected-support process theorem constructs a centered Gaussian field

```math
\mathbb G_C(t;\varphi),
\qquad
\varphi\in\mathcal M_C,
```

linear in $\varphi$, with covariance

```math
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbb G_C(s;\varphi)
\mathbb G_D(t;\psi)
\right]
=
\mathbf 1_{\{C\cong D\}}
|\operatorname{Aut}(C)|
\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle
\min(s,t)^{v(C)}.
```

Fields belonging to nonisomorphic connected patterns are independent. For a fixed $C$, the process is Brownian motion in the Hilbert space $\mathcal M_C$, run at time $t^{v(C)}$.

For a pure component tensor

```math
g_P
=
\operatorname{Sym}_P
\left(
\varphi_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\varphi_m
\right),
```

define its Gaussian Wick lift by

```math
\mathbb W_P(t;g_P)
=
\operatorname{Sym}_P
:
\mathbb G_{C_1}(t;\varphi_1)
\cdots
\mathbb G_{C_m}(t;\varphi_m)
:.
```

The colons denote the Wick product. The definition extends by linearity and $L^2$ completion to every $g_P\in\mathcal M_P$.

The normalization of the connected fields and the component symmetrization give the Wick isometry

```math
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbb W_P(s;g_P)
\mathbb W_Q(t;h_Q)
\right]
=
\mathbf 1_{\{P\cong Q\}}
|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|
\langle g_P,h_P\rangle
\min(s,t)^{v(P)}.
```

For two identical one-loop components and a unit kernel, this is the familiar identity

```math
\mathbb W_P(t)=B(t)^2-t.
```

Its variance is $2t^2$, equal to $|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|t^2$.

# 3. Sequential Wick limit

For a canonical kernel $g_P$, retain the ordered embedding sum

```math
S_{n,P}(g_P)
=
\sum_{\psi:V(P)\hookrightarrow[n]}
(g_P)_\psi.
```

## Theorem 2. Functional limit for arbitrary support topology

For every looped support pattern $P$ on $d$ vertices,

```math
N^{-d/2}
S_{\lfloor Nt\rfloor,P}(g_P)
\Longrightarrow
\mathbb W_P(t;g_P)
```

jointly on compact time intervals. Limits belonging to nonisomorphic support patterns are orthogonal, and their joint dependence is generated entirely by shared connected Gaussian component fields.

## Proof

It is enough first to consider a finite symmetrized tensor expansion. For a pure tensor, multiply the connected embedding sums associated with $C_1,\ldots,C_m$. The product includes all choices of component embeddings, including choices in which two components share vertices or primitive coordinates.

The statistic $S_{n,P}(g_P)$ retains only mutually disjoint component embeddings. Möbius inversion on the partition lattice of component collisions expresses this disjoint sum as the product of connected sums minus all collision contractions. Under the normalization $N^{-d/2}$, the connected functional limits convert the product and its contractions into the Gaussian Wick product.

For two one-loop components, the identity is exact:

```math
\sum_{i\neq j}\varphi(X_i)\varphi(X_j)
=
\left(\sum_i\varphi(X_i)\right)^2
-
\sum_i\varphi(X_i)^2.
```

After normalization, the second term becomes the quadratic-variation subtraction in

```math
:B(t)^2:
=
B(t)^2-t.
```

The same partition-lattice cancellation gives the general Wick polynomial. Every collision joining components lowers the number of freely embedded vertices by the amount required for the corresponding Gaussian contraction; configurations with additional identifications are lower order.

The connected component processes converge jointly, so the continuous-mapping theorem gives convergence of each finite tensor expansion. The exact support-pattern variance identity controls the error of an $L^2$ approximation uniformly on compact time intervals, using the martingale maximal inequality as in the connected case. This completes the extension to arbitrary $g_P$.

This is the sequential form of the generalized $U$-statistic limit indexed by the connected components of the principal support graph.

# 4. Stationary logarithmic Wick fields

Define the stationary connected fields

```math
\mathbb X_C(s;\varphi)
=
 e^{-v(C)s/2}
\mathbb G_C(e^s;\varphi).
```

Each $\mathbb X_C$ is an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck field with relaxation rate $v(C)/2$. For a support pattern $P$ of total order $d$, set

```math
\mathbb V_P(s;g_P)
=
 e^{-ds/2}
\mathbb W_P(e^s;g_P).
```

For a component tensor, this is equivalently

```math
\mathbb V_P(s;g_P)
=
\operatorname{Sym}_P
:
\mathbb X_{C_1}(s;\varphi_1)
\cdots
\mathbb X_{C_m}(s;\varphi_m)
:.
```

## Corollary 3. Stationary support-pattern process

For

```math
X_{N,P}(s)
=
m_N(s)^{d/2}
U_{m_N(s),P}(g_P),
\qquad
m_N(s)=\lfloor Ne^s\rfloor,
```

we have

```math
X_{N,P}\Longrightarrow\mathbb V_P.
```

The limiting process is stationary and satisfies

```math
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbb V_P(s;g_P)
\mathbb V_Q(t;h_Q)
\right]
=
\mathbf 1_{\{P\cong Q\}}
|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|
\langle g_P,h_P\rangle
 e^{-v(P)|s-t|/2}.
```

The process is Gaussian exactly when its Wick lift lies in the first chaos of the connected field family. A connected pattern always has this property. A disconnected pattern generally produces a higher Gaussian chaos or a product of independent Gaussian fields.

The process $\mathbb V_P$ need not be Markov by itself. It is, however, an observable of the joint Markov family of connected OU fields.

# 5. Full finite-pattern functional theorem

Let $f$ be any symmetric square-integrable joint vertex-edge kernel on $k$ vertices. Let $d=d(f)$ be its principal vertex-support degree and $f_P$ its canonical support projections.

## Theorem 4. Complete principal-support continuum limit

The logarithmically rescaled statistic

```math
Z_{f,N}(s)
=
m_N(s)^{d/2}
\left(
T_{m_N(s)}(f)-\theta_f
\right)
```

converges to

```math
Z_f(s)
=
\sum_{\substack{P\\v(P)=d}}
\frac{(k)_d}{|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|}
\mathbb V_P(s;f_P).
```

For two kernels $f$ and $g$ with the same principal degree $d$,

```math
\mathbb E[Z_f(s)Z_g(t)]
=
C_d(f,g)e^{-d|s-t|/2},
```

where

```math
C_d(f,g)
=
\sum_{\substack{P\\v(P)=d}}
\frac{(k)_d(\ell)_d}{|\operatorname{Aut}(P)|}
\mathbb E[f_Pg_P].
```

## Proof

The exact decomposition separates the principal order $d$ from all higher support orders. The latter vanish in $L^2$ under the normalization $m_N^{d/2}$. Apply Theorem 2 to each principal support projection and multiply by its exact orbit coefficient. Orthogonality of nonisomorphic supports and the Wick isometry give the covariance formula.

In every finite-layer finite-pattern sector, the sum contains finitely many finite-dimensional canonical components. The process limit is therefore an explicit polynomial in finitely many connected OU fields.

This theorem completes the functional continuum limit for the entire finite-pattern algebra of the dissociated ordered-kernel sectors.

# 6. Weighted OU spectral law

Let $\mathcal L$ be the generator of the joint connected OU field. A coordinate belonging to a connected support $C$ has relaxation eigenvalue

```math
\frac{v(C)}2.
```

Wick products are eigenfunctions of the second-quantized generator. If

```math
P=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_m,
```

then

```math
\mathcal L\mathbb V_P
=
-\frac{1}{2}
\left(
\sum_{a=1}^m v(C_a)
\right)
\mathbb V_P
=
-\frac{v(P)}{2}\mathbb V_P.
```

## Corollary 5. Support order is the exact relaxation eigenvalue

Every principal support process of order $d$, connected or disconnected, belongs to the generator eigenspace with eigenvalue

```math
-\frac d2.
```

Thus the previously observed rule is now a process and semigroup theorem:

```math
\boxed{
\text{principal vertex support }d
\Longrightarrow
\text{normalization }n^{d/2}
\Longrightarrow
\text{OU-field eigenvalue }-\frac d2
}
```

Connectedness determines whether the eigen-observable itself is a Gaussian OU coordinate. Disconnectedness determines the Wick polynomial within the same eigenspace.

This explains why the non-Gaussian quadratic mark mode and the Gaussian pair-noise mode in the symmetric two-layer edge density have identical covariance decay. Both are eigen-observables with total support degree two, even though one is a Wick square of rate-$1/2$ fields and the other is a primitive rate-one field.

# 7. Loop-plus-edge example

Let $(S_i)$ and $(\xi_{ij})$ be independent Rademacher families. Consider the canonical support pattern consisting of one loop component and one ordinary-edge component, with kernel

```math
g_P=S_1\xi_{23}.
```

The pattern has three supporting vertices and automorphism group of order two, generated by exchanging the endpoints of the edge. Its ordered embedding sum is

```math
S_{n,P}
=
\sum_{\substack{i,j,k\\\text{distinct}}}
S_i\xi_{jk}.
```

Let $X^{(1)}$ be the unit-variance rate-$1/2$ OU field generated by the loop component, and let $X^{(2)}$ be the variance-two rate-one OU field generated by the ordered edge component. The logarithmic limit is

```math
\mathbb V_P(s)
=
X^{(1)}(s)X^{(2)}(s).
```

The factors are independent. Hence

```math
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb V_P)=2,
```

and

```math
\mathbb E
\left[
\mathbb V_P(s)\mathbb V_P(t)
\right]
=
2e^{-3|s-t|/2}.
```

The standardized marginal kurtosis is $9$, rather than the Gaussian value $3$. The support is disconnected, so the limit is a product-normal field, but its total support order still gives the relaxation rate $3/2$.

# 8. Recovery of the preceding examples

For two disconnected one-loop components, Corollary 3 gives

```math
:X(s)^2:
=
X(s)^2-\mathbb E[X(s)^2],
```

with rate $1$. This is the quadratic layer-proportion term in the symmetric two-layer edge density.

For the equal-weight three-layer route statistic, the disconnected two-loop projection gives the quadratic mark term, while the connected edge projections give primitive rate-one OU fields. Theorem 4 produces their sum in one principal support eigenspace.

For a connected three-vertex path of pair variables, the component count is one, so the Wick lift is first-order in its own connected Gaussian field. The limit is therefore Gaussian with rate $3/2$, in agreement with the connected-support process theorem.

These examples are not separate fluctuation mechanisms. They are the connected-component cases of one support-pattern continuum theorem.

# 9. Functional continuum consequences

Within every recovered finite ordered-kernel sector, canonical looped-support projections decompose each finite vertex-edge statistic into connected-component Ornstein-Uhlenbeck fields and a Wick-polynomial stationary limit.

The stable coefficients, normalization, covariance decay, distributional type, and generator eigenvalue are all computable from the finite primitive-coordinate decomposition.

The remaining continuum questions lie outside this finite dissociated setting. They include infinite or nonparametric central sectors, sparse sectors, support orders that grow with system size, and graph states whose extension law is not representable by independent vertex and pair variables with a fixed finite kernel.

# 10. Literature relation

S. Janson and K. Nowicki, *The asymptotic distributions of generalized U-statistics with applications to random graphs*, Probability Theory and Related Fields 90 (1991), 341-375, proved that arbitrary symmetric generalized $U$-statistics have limits described by Wick products of Gaussian variables associated with the connected components of their principal support graphs. Their two-component theorem gives the familiar independent Gaussian plus centered chi-square form, and their general theorem gives higher Wick products.

The present derivation adds the nested-level and logarithmic-size process structure. Each connected component becomes a time-changed Brownian field, and its Lamperti transform is an OU field weighted by the component's vertex count. Wick multiplication then preserves the total relaxation eigenvalue $v(P)/2$.

The resulting functional theorem is the process-level counterpart of the generalized $U$-statistic support-graph classification.
