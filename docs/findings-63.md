# 1. Relative finite selection

The noncompression theorem of `findings-60.md` excludes one finite pattern
list that identifies every extremal central DAG sector, while exact finite
selection inside a fixed finite-dimensional model class remains available.
The ordered-block sectors provide such classes, and their finite-pattern
coordinates are polynomial in the model parameters.

Polynomial identifiability by the complete profile reduces to
identifiability by a finite pattern list. The result is relative to the
chosen model class and does not assert that the same constraints exclude
every sector outside that class.

# 2. Polynomial profile families

Let $K\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be a parameter set. Suppose that every
$\vartheta\in K$ determines an extremal coherent DAG profile

```math
\mu^{\vartheta}
```

and that, for every finite DAG $F$, the induced density

```math
P_F(\vartheta)
=
\mu^{\vartheta}_{|F|}(F)
```

is the restriction to $K$ of a polynomial in $\vartheta$.

The family is profile-identifiable when

```math
P_F(\vartheta)=P_F(\vartheta')
\quad\text{for every finite }F
```

implies $\vartheta=\vartheta'$.

## Theorem 1. Noetherian finite separation

Every profile-identifiable polynomial family is separated by finitely
many finite-DAG densities. More precisely, there exist finite DAGs

```math
F_1,\ldots,F_M
```

such that

```math
P_{F_a}(\vartheta)=P_{F_a}(\vartheta')
\quad
(1\leq a\leq M)
```

implies $\vartheta=\vartheta'$ for all $\vartheta,\vartheta'\in K$.

## Proof

Introduce two copies of the parameter variables,

```math
x=(x_1,\ldots,x_d),
\qquad
y=(y_1,\ldots,y_d),
```

and consider the ideal

```math
I
=
\left\langle
P_F(x)-P_F(y):F\text{ a finite DAG}
\right\rangle
```

in the polynomial ring

```math
\mathbb R[x_1,\ldots,x_d,y_1,\ldots,y_d].
```

Hilbert's basis theorem makes this ring Noetherian, so $I$ is finitely
generated. Each finite generator belongs to the ideal generated by the
displayed pattern differences and is therefore a finite polynomial
combination of finitely many of them. Taking the union of those finite
collections gives patterns $F_1,\ldots,F_M$ for which

```math
I
=
\left\langle
P_{F_a}(x)-P_{F_a}(y):1\leq a\leq M
\right\rangle.
```

Suppose that the selected coordinates agree at $(\vartheta,\vartheta')$.
Every polynomial in $I$ then vanishes at this pair. In particular,

```math
P_F(\vartheta)-P_F(\vartheta')=0
```

for every finite DAG $F$. Profile identifiability gives $\vartheta=\vartheta'$.

## Corollary 2. Finite relational selection within a model class

For every fixed $\vartheta_0\in K$, the finite constraints

```math
P_{F_a}(\vartheta)
=
P_{F_a}(\vartheta_0),
\qquad
1\leq a\leq M,
```

select $\vartheta_0$ uniquely among the sectors in the family.

Thus a finite-dimensional algebraic sector class admits a finite
relational coordinate system even though the complete central boundary
does not.

# 3. Ordered stochastic block DAGs

Fix $q\geq2$. Let the parameter consist of

```math
\vartheta=(p,W),
```

where

```math
p_i>0,
\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^q p_i=1,
```

and

```math
0\leq w_{ij}\leq1,
\qquad
1\leq i<j\leq q.
```

Assume positive adjacent connectivity,

```math
w_{i,i+1}>0,
\qquad
1\leq i<q.
```

Every finite induced-DAG probability is polynomial in $p_i$, $w_{ij}$,
and $1-w_{ij}$: one sums over the finitely many assignments of the
pattern vertices to ordered layers and multiplies the required edge and
nonedge probabilities.

The complete profile is injective on this parameter class. By
`findings-51.md`, a sample almost surely recovers the ordered layer
partition after a finite random level. The layer frequencies then recover
$p$, and the block-edge frequencies recover $W$. Distinct parameters
therefore induce distinct exchangeable DAG laws.

## Corollary 3. Finite separation of every fixed ordered-block family

For every fixed $q$, there is a finite collection of DAG patterns whose
densities identify the complete parameter pair $(p,W)$ throughout the
positive-adjacent $q$-layer family.

The number and size of the required patterns may increase with $q$. This
is consistent with `findings-60.md`, because there is no one fixed list
that works for all $q$.

# 4. Explicit two-layer coordinates

The two-layer family gives a small explicit instance. Write

```math
p_1=\theta,
\qquad
p_2=1-\theta,
\qquad
w_{12}=r,
```

with $0<\theta,r<1$. Let $E$ be the two-vertex one-edge DAG. Let $S^+$
be the three-vertex out-star with one source and two sinks, and let
$S^-$ be the three-vertex in-star with two sources and one sink.

Their induced densities are

```math
P_E
=
2\theta(1-\theta)r,
```

the out-star density is

```math
P_{S^+}
=
3\theta(1-\theta)^2r^2,
```

and

```math
P_{S^-}
=
3\theta^2(1-\theta)r^2.
```

Since the star densities are positive,

```math
\theta
=
\frac{P_{S^-}}{P_{S^-}+P_{S^+}},
```

and

```math
r
=
\frac{P_E}{2\theta(1-\theta)}.
```

Thus three elementary relational frequencies recover the entire
two-layer sector.

# 5. Robust finite identification

Assume now that $K$ is compact. Let

```math
Q(\vartheta)
=
\left(
P_{F_1}(\vartheta),\ldots,P_{F_M}(\vartheta)
\right).
```

Theorem 1 makes $Q$ continuous and injective. A continuous injection
from a compact space into a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism onto its
image.

## Proposition 4. Uniform stability of selected coordinates

For every $\epsilon>0$, there exists $\delta>0$ such that

```math
\|Q(\vartheta)-Q(\vartheta')\|<\delta
```

implies

```math
\|\vartheta-\vartheta'\|<\epsilon
```

for all $\vartheta,\vartheta'\in K$.

Consequently, the finite identifying pattern list is exact and uniformly
stable on every compact positive parameter region. Combining this
statement with the predictive stability theorem of `findings-61.md`
gives uniform finite-horizon stability of the reconstructed up kernels
away from zero-mass states.

# 6. Projective invariance

For fixed $q$, the ordered-block parameter space maps into the extremal
central boundary as a finite-dimensional projectively invariant family.
Restriction from level $n+1$ to level $n$ preserves the same parameter
$(p,W)$, and every level distribution and upward kernel is obtained
from that parameter.

The family therefore supplies a finite-dimensional sector state:

```math
(p,W)
\longmapsto
\mu^{p,W}
\longmapsto
U^{p,W}.
```

The endpoint DAG remains the relational microstate. The finite parameter
records the selected coherent sector within this restricted class.

# 7. Exact rational checks

Across a rational grid in the positive two-layer sector, exact arithmetic
shows that the edge, out-star, and in-star densities recover $(\theta,r)$.
The complete induced three-vertex distribution is normalized and agrees
with the stated formulas.

# 8. Literature relation

The proof uses the Noetherianity of polynomial rings rather than a
graphon-specific forcing theorem. It is compatible with the theory of
finitely forcible graphons of L. Lovász and B. Szegedy, *Finitely
forcible graphons*, Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B 101 (2011),
269-301, arXiv:0901.0929, where prescribed finite subgraph densities
can isolate individual limit objects. The present theorem is deliberately
relative: it gives finite identification inside an algebraic central
family and does not claim absolute forcibility in the full digraphon
space.
